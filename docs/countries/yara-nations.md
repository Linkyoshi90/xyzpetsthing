> **Respect note — read before drawing anything.** This country is modelled on living Aboriginal cultures. The lore's own rules apply to art direction: some knowledge is restricted, some places are men's or women's business, and permission comes first. Practically: **invent all motifs**, never imitate dot painting, cross-hatching (rarrk), clan designs, or specific rock-art imagery; never depict ceremony or sacred sites; never put sacred-object shapes on tradeable items. When in doubt, show the *place* rather than the *design*.

## Design thesis
**The first law is Country.** Warraluma is built around keeping places and meeting grounds rather than walls — boardwalks over wetland, shade sails over red earth, painted posts marking paths that follow songlines. Design should express *stewardship*: things marked, tended, seasonally read, and left better than found.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Painted wayfinding post** marking a path bend where a plant, animal, or season is taught
- Shade sail stitched over a plaza; boardwalk skimming wetland
- Ranger's tide and croc board at the river steps
- Smoking ceremony and cool-burn line moving carefully through grass (depict the *practice*, from a respectful distance)
- The curlew call that means go home by the lit path
- Star Field horizon talk — the dark-shape constellation in the Milky Way
- Country fund box on a market counter

## Pattern and ornament
- Ground-and-line marking as a *concept*: paths, boundaries, and seasonal marks laid on earth. **Invent the vocabulary; do not copy real designs.**
- Woven fibre baskets and dilly-bag texture
- Fish-trap and net lattice
- Paperbark layering; mud-crack texture on dry ground
- Tide script: mirror ripples writing themselves at the river mouth

## Materials and finish
Timber boardwalk, paperbark, woven fibre, shell, stone, canvas shade, ochre pigment, steel ranger kit, cool water. Finish: **sun-hardened and dust-dulled**, with wet-season green as the seasonal swing.

## Architecture and silhouette
Open circles rather than enclosures: a red-earth meeting ground with shade trees, timber river terraces, a cultural centre and archive, a long thatched market roof, raised mangrove walkways. Silhouette: **low shade structures under a huge escarpment and a huger sky**.

## Dress and accessories
Contemporary practical clothing — work shirts, hats, boots, ranger hi-vis — with cultural pieces worn by right, not as costume. Accessories: water bottle, radio, first-aid kit, woven basket, clapsticks (as instruments in the market context).

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Barramundi in paperbark, kangaroo skewers with pepperberry, damper with wattleseed, bush tomatoes, quandong tarts, lemon myrtle iced tea, woven baskets, shell work, prints, and the counters at the Warraluma Keeping Place Shop. Signage is **plain lettering plus painted symbol posts**, with clear access notices (some rooms are restricted — show the sign).

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 18`): **Bunyip, Cassowrath, Gliderune, Rainbow Serpent, Spoodr, Tiddalik**.

**Handle Rainbow Serpent with particular care** — it is a sacred figure to many real nations. Keep the in-world design abstract and elemental (water, light refraction, river shape) rather than an illustration of any real tradition, and consider treating it as a weather/landscape event rather than a battleable creature. Bunyip belongs to the billabong: heard, rippling, mostly unseen. Cassowrath and Spoodr are the genuinely dangerous locals. Gliderune is the small, warm one.

## Guardrails
- **Invent every motif.** No dot painting, no rarrk cross-hatching, no reproduced rock art.
- Show permission structures in the art: signs, custodians, guided access, closed rooms. That is the culture, not an obstacle to it.
- Contemporary and self-governing. Rangers have radios; the archive has a reading table; the calendar has many seasons, not four.
