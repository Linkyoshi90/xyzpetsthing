> **Respect note:** the lore draws on living Pacific cultures (marae, tapa, kava, star compass) and already handles them with care — ask before entering, shoes off, never step on mats, a canoe is family. Keep designs **invented within the world**; do not reproduce real tapa patterns, tā moko, or specific ceremonial carvings.

## Design thesis
**The stars are street signs and the tide is the town clock.** Navakai is lashed, woven, and open — almost nothing here is solid wall. The signature idea is *navigation*: a packed-sand circle marked with swell lines and star houses, where the whole town tips its face to the sky at night.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Star compass** — a sand circle marked with swell lines and star houses; the country's emblem
- Outrigger canoe on a timber cradle (a canoe is family — treat it as a character)
- Conch shell horn that calls meetings and dance practice
- Lashed sennit joints: the cordage binding posts is *the* ornament
- Tide board at the quay; a bell for the reef path
- Woven mat as a social boundary (never step on one)
- Reef Gate: pale stakes marking the coral gap

## Pattern and ornament
- Plaited and woven geometry — the weave itself is the pattern (pandanus, coconut fibre)
- Barkcloth-style stamped bands: repeating triangles, chevrons, and grids in earth pigment
- Carved post figures with deep chiselled relief (invent the vocabulary)
- Shell inlay dots and rows
- Lashing patterns: X-wraps and spiral bindings shown proudly, never hidden

## Materials and finish
Timber posts, sennit cord, pandanus and coconut fibre, thatch, barkcloth, shell, bone, coral lime, palm oil, dye bowls. Finish: **oiled, smoke-darkened, and salt-cured**; nothing painted glossy, everything hand-finished.

## Architecture and silhouette
Open-sided longhouses (fale) of posts and lashings with lifted rafters, a sacred courtyard terrace, canoe slips and fish racks, a lookout ridge. Silhouette: **broad low roofs on open posts** — you can see through most buildings.

## Dress and accessories
Wrapped cloth at the waist, barkcloth and woven pieces for ceremony, flower and shell adornment, oiled skin against sun and salt. Accessories: fibre baskets, fishing line reels, a paddle, a shell horn, a nose flute from Tapa Walk.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Fish racks, nets, spice packets from the dock, taro and breadfruit from the earth oven, coconut cups, hibiscus tea, kava half-shells, dye bowls "sitting like small moons". Signage is **carved or woven marks and colour**, not text.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 11`): **Adaro, Cocoskink, Crab man, Frondbat, Moo, Nenekea, Nightmarcher, Taniwha**.

Reef and grove creatures plus ocean spirits. Taniwha and Adaro are the water powers — build them from swell shapes and fish anatomy rather than from dragons. Nightmarcher is the solemn one: a procession seen at distance, torchlight and silhouette only, never confronted. Give the small ones woven cordage, shell inlay markings, and reef-colour palettes.

## Landmarks to draw from
Star Compass Green · Marae Terrace · Lagoon Quay · Fale Longhouse · Tapa Walk · Reef Gate · Palm Ridge · Navakai Spice Dock

## Guardrails
- **Lashings visible, walls absent.** Solid enclosed buildings read wrong here.
- The sacred courtyard is a place with rules — depict it respectfully and from outside where appropriate.
- Distinguish from Pelagora: Navakai is warm, living, and open; Pelagora is cold stone and half-drowned.
