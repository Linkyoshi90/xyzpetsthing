## Design thesis
**Law is the lamp and mercy the oil.** A city of books and bales where the most beautiful objects are public utilities: fountains, bread ovens, schools, shade. Design should feel *measured* — cool tile, cool air, careful weights — with warmth entering only through lamplight and hospitality.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Crescent** over a gate arch — the identity mark
- **Windcatcher (malqaf) tower** — a distinctive skyline shape unique to this country in the setting
- Fountain with a brass cup at every quarter (waqf-funded)
- Public tile boards posting fines, accounts, and endowments
- The muhtasib's balance and test weights
- Kite strings running tower to tower in the north wind
- Caravanserai gate with a brass bell rung by late arrivals

## Pattern and ornament
- Geometric girih tiling: 8- and 10-point stars, interlaced strapwork, no figures
- Muqarnas (stalactite vaulting) in gateway hoods and niche heads
- Blue-and-white tile panels with vegetal arabesque
- Calligraphic shop signs — invented script, treat as ornament, flowing and horizontal
- Canvas stripe: high market awnings in wide saffron/white or indigo/white bands

## Materials and finish
Glazed tile, carved plaster, mudbrick, cedar screens (mashrabiya), brass and copper, carpets, dyed wool, leather, paper and vellum, glass. Finish is **cool, matte, and shaded** with polished metal accents; the desert light never hits a surface directly if the architecture can help it.

## Architecture and silhouette
Crescent walls, minarets, iwans (great vaulted porches), courtyard madrasas, domed bathhouses, a caravanserai with a stone court and stables, and a street of windcatchers. Silhouette: **domes and slender towers over flat rooftop terraces**, plus the boxy windcatcher heads.

## Dress and accessories
Layered robes and wide wraps, head coverings for sun and modesty, indigo and undyed linen for the road, finer stripe and embroidery in town. Accessories: brass cup, ink case at the belt, prayer beads, a travel kettle, a merchant's tally book, a folded carpet strapped to a camel.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Astronomer's astrolabe and rooftop terrace, brass lamps, spice trays, glass phials at the Moon-and-Mortar Apothecary, sesame rings, cardamom coffee, sharbat of rose and mint, bookbinder's tools at Dar al Qalam. Signage is **calligraphic on tile or carved wood**.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 12`): **Bahamut, Camel, Caraviper, Censer Lion, Dunefennec, Genie, Manticore**.

Caravan fauna plus grand myth. Give the desert animals travel hardware — woven blankets, brass bells, water skins. Censer Lion is the design keystone: a lion whose mane is smoke from a hanging brass censer. Genie and Bahamut should be *architectural* in scale, built from tile pattern and smoke rather than muscle.

## Landmarks to draw from
Qalb al Qanun (Law Quarter) · Souq al Hilal · Dar al Qalam · Khan al Safar · Qanat Gardens · Towers of the Wind · Bayt al Waqf · Moon-and-Mortar Apothecary

## Guardrails
- **Geometry and vegetation, not figures**, in architectural ornament — the aniconic convention is the look.
- Invented script only. Never render real Qur'anic text as decoration or on items.
- The country card jokes about oil and sheikhs; the lore is a 15th-century scholarship-and-caravan city. Follow the lore.
- Charity infrastructure (fountains, ovens, endowment boards) is the civic pride — put it in the frame.
