## Design thesis
**Show up, vote often, coach youth sports, keep the crosswalks painted.** Meridian Arc is the setting's civic-modern anchor: brick and glass, straight street trees, signage everywhere, and paperwork for the shadows too. It is the most *legible* city in Harmontide — everything is labelled.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Statehouse dome** on a low skyline
- Arc-light: the tall street lamp that gives the capital its name
- Steel-vaulted station concourse with a departure board
- Painted crosswalk and bike lane, treated as civic pride
- Mural vs. billboard, arguing on the same block
- Posted budgets on the station board; saplings handed out on civic days
- The Arc Line couriers' receipts — even the shadow economy files a spreadsheet

## Pattern and ornament
- Brick bond and warehouse window grids (steel sash, many small panes)
- Ghost-sign lettering fading on brick
- Highway and transit sign systems: shields, arrows, sans-serif, high contrast
- Diner chrome-and-vinyl striping; neon tube outlines
- Bunting and pennant strings for game days and market days

## Materials and finish
Brick, steel, glass curtain wall, poured concrete, chrome, vinyl, enamel signage, asphalt, string lights, painted plywood. Finish: **used and maintained** — salt-stained in winter, mural-fresh in summer.

## Architecture and silhouette
Statehouse and courts on one end, brick warehouse district on the other, a riverwalk with a ballpark and concert shell, brownstone stoops, a grand station under a steel vault, a signature bridge with a pedestrian deck. Silhouette: **flat-topped blocks plus one dome and one bridge span**.

## Dress and accessories
Contemporary: work jackets, hoodies, team caps, tote bags, lanyards, body cams on the Civic Department. Union patches and start-up badges on the same street. Accessories: transit card, coffee cup, folding bike, dog leash.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Diner counters and bottomless coffee, food-truck menus, deli sandwiches, brewery flights, carousel tokens, holiday light strings, corner-mart shelves (`?pg=urb-corner-mart`). Signage is **the richest in the setting**: enamel transit plates, neon, ghost signs, hand-chalked specials, printed vinyl.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 22`): **Death, Fury, Gaitress, Jack-o-Lantern, Jackalope, Lich, Mad Hatter, Meloncollie, Mimic, Pestilence, Pumpkingull, Slidred, Snowloper, Tin Jack, War**.

The largest and strangest roster — Americana tall-tales, Halloween figures, and all four Horsemen (War, Fury, Pestilence, Famine sits with STAP, Death here). Treat it as **the country's costume-and-carnival streak**: roadside-attraction Jackalope, seasonal Pumpkingull and Jack-o-Lantern, tin-toy Tin Jack. The Horsemen should be the tonal outliers — plain, quiet, and modern-dressed rather than gothic.

## Guardrails
- Modern means modern: phones, cameras, cards, spreadsheets, body cams.
- Keep the tone good-humoured and civic. The horror lives in Stillwater Hollow, not here.
- Distinguish from Gran Columbia: URB is brick-and-steel and cold winters; Solvine is glass-and-hillside and sun.
