## Design thesis
**A ledger drawn on water.** Straight dikes, square fields, and lock-gates that turn a landscape into arithmetic. Where Rheingard measures with bells and books, Frankenondermeer measures with water gauges and flood marks. The mood is patient, damp, and quietly superstitious: the canals are said to remember your temper.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Flood mark** — a dated line cut or painted on a tower, wall, or post. Use it as the region's stamp.
- The lock-gate stair and its sluice wheels
- Windmill and tide-sail arms, painted in house colours
- Sounding pole and a stamp-sealed notebook (Dikewarden kit)
- Chalk blessing marks on mill beams at the First Pumping
- Reeds bent by wind — the "reedsong" that repeats your worries back softer
- Three short bell or horn notes: lock closed, do not approach

## Pattern and ornament
- Long horizontal banding: dike, road, field, canal — repeat it in every composition
- Painted house-colour bands on mill arms and boat hulls (two colours, hard edge)
- Brick bond patterns and stepped/crow-stepped gable outlines
- Delft-style blue-on-white tile panels for shop interiors and item icons
- Reed thatch texture; woven rush mats

## Materials and finish
Brick, tarred timber piles, canvas, rope, peat, painted boat wood, pewter, tin lanterns, glazed tile. Everything is wet at the base with a clean dry line above it — barnacle-free but stained by tide.

## Architecture and silhouette
Narrow stepped-gable brick houses leaning over a canal, crane-beams jutting from attics, low bridges, an enormous stair of lock gates, a spire on an artificial mound used as a flood mark. Silhouette: **flat horizon plus tall thin verticals** (mills, spires, crane beams).

## Dress and accessories
Practical layers: oiled canvas coats, blue-lined cloaks for the Dikewardens, wool caps, clogs or heavy boots left at thresholds, reflector charms and lanterns after dusk. Accessories are tools: sounding pole, tide table card, ink-stained cuffs, a notebook on a cord.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Water gauges, open ledgers, barge-market awnings, cheese wheels, dried fish, pickled eel, syrup and rye, canal-flowers, boat charms. Signage is **painted board plus a tide/level number** — numbers matter more than words here.

## Creature design cues
Frankenondermeer has no separate species table; it draws on Rheinland's roster. Favour the **water-facing** members — Bullywug, Mailtoad, Papillon, Coin Magpie — and re-skin with canal colours, rope harness, tin lantern charms, and reed patterning. Mood-linked spirits are canon here: the lore says willful currents rise when tempers do, so a Frankenondermeer variant can read as a Rheinland creature with water-slick, mood-reactive markings.

## Guardrails
- Keep it *low*: the horizon should sit high in the frame and the sky should do the work.
- Mist here is functional (fog thickens when feelings run high), unlike Bretonreach's fae mist.
- Don't add mountains, cliffs, or Rheingard's spiky vertical skyline.
