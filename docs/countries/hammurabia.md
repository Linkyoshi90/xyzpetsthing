## Design thesis
**The river teaches justice: measure fairly, keep channels clear, do not move your neighbour's marker stone.** Ziggurab is a city of tablets and toll-gates where everything worth doing gets written into clay and everything worth owning gets weighed. Sun-baked brick with sudden shocks of blue glaze.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Ziggurat stair** — terraced, stepping to a small shrine. The country's whole silhouette in one shape.
- **Clay tablet with wedge script** — the item stamp, the sign board, the legal record
- Glazed-brick processional gate with relief lions and bulls
- Public balance beam at the Gate of Lions, checked at noon
- Marker stone (do not move it) and the re-cut measuring rod at new year
- Floodmarks carved with the year's height
- Tide board at Bitumen Wharf — the city's metronome

## Pattern and ornament
- Wedge (cuneiform) bands used as decorative border and as real text
- Cone-mosaic geometry: coloured cone-heads pressed into plaster in zigzags and lozenges
- Rosettes and guilloche on glazed panels
- Stepped crenellation along the tops of walls
- Woven reed matting texture; flounced tiered fabric on formal dress

## Materials and finish
Mudbrick, glazed brick, clay, bitumen, reed, copper and bronze, wool, date wood, carnelian and lapis for small goods. Finish: **matte and dusty** everywhere, with a small number of high-gloss glazed surfaces that should feel genuinely expensive.

## Architecture and silhouette
A great stepped temple at the centre, terraced districts, canal seams between quarters, low wharves, vaulted granary barns, mudbrick courtyard houses under palms. Silhouette: **a stepped pyramid against horizontal city banding**.

## Dress and accessories
Tiered flounced skirts and shawls for formal wear, plain wrapped kilts and tunics for work. Accessories: a cylinder seal worn on a cord (roll it to sign), a reed stylus, a copper cup, a rod-and-cord measuring set for gaugers, sandals for dust.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Clay tablets and seal impressions, sealed clay packets, measuring rods, barley beer in clay cups, date syrup kettles, flatbreads, reed skiffs, the goods at the Ziggurat Ledger House. Signage is **stele-shaped**: an upright stone slab with carved text and a figure at the top.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 13`): **Bronzejaw Ox, Dugong, Girtablilu, Ibisking, Lamassu, Oryx, Reedbull**.

Gate-guardian energy. Lamassu and Girtablilu are the anchors: composite beings that look **carved** — squared beards, tiered feather wings, stone-relief posing. Give the herd animals bronze fittings and glazed-blue caparisons. Dugong and Reedbull belong to the marsh side: reed, silt, and water-line staining.

## Landmarks to draw from
Stair of the Stars · Tablet House · Gate of Lions · Canal of Measures · Bitumen Wharf · Date Grove Quarter · House of Weights and Rods

## Guardrails
- Everything is **measured**: put a rod, a balance, a floodmark, or a tally into most compositions.
- Distinguish from Kemet: Hammurabia is brick, blue glaze, and wedges; Kemet is stone, painted pylons, and hieroglyph bands.
- Keep the glaze rare. If everything is lapis, nothing is.
