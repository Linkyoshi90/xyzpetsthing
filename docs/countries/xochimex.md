## Design thesis
**A living market and a memory palace at once.** Modern towers and metro lines threaded over ruins and canals, with flowers doing the emotional work. Where the Eagle Serpent Dominion is the ceremonial past, Xochimex is the loud, generous, present-day descendant living on top of it.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Papel picado** — cut-paper banners strung across a street. Use it as the country's border/frame element.
- Marigold arch over a painted boat (trajinera) with a name across the bow
- Lucha mask: bold symmetrical colour blocks and eye shapes
- Altar tier with candles, bread, photographs, flowers
- Stone serpent head half-embedded in a modern wall (old stones under the grid)
- Fruit cup with lime and chile; the market lunch counter
- Metro line colours used as wayfinding

## Pattern and ornament
- Talavera tile: blue-on-white florals with yellow and green accents
- Papel picado silhouettes (skulls, birds, flowers, lettering) cut from flat colour
- Mural style: hard black outline, flat saturated fill, portrait scale
- Woven serape stripe bands, brighter and warmer than Sapa Inti weaving
- Tiled house-number and street-name plaques

## Materials and finish
Painted concrete, tile, tissue paper, marigold and cempasúchil bundles, sheet metal awnings, plastic crates, volcanic stone, enamel-painted boat wood, sequins and lamé for masks and capes. Finish: **saturated, layered, and a bit sun-battered**.

## Architecture and silhouette
Monumental plaza with colonnades, boulevards with roundabout monuments, a covered market city-within-a-city, canal boroughs with chinampas, a wrestling arena, a hill lookout. Silhouette: **wide flat plazas and long boulevards with a distant volcano rim**.

## Dress and accessories
Everyday modern with festival layers: jackets and jeans most days, then embroidered blouses, rebozos, sequinned lucha capes, flower crowns, and face paint on calendar days. Vendors wear aprons with coin pockets; boat pilots wear straw hats.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Tamales and atole, tacos al pastor, churros, pozole, fruit cups, marigold bundles, paper banners, wrestling masks, museum tickets, trajinera cushions. Signage is **hand-painted vendor lettering with heavy drop shadow**, next to modern transit sans-serif.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 8`): **Axolume, Charro Negro, Chupacabra, Coatimano, La Llorona, La Mano Peluda**.

This is the setting's **urban-legend** roster — three of six are ghost-story figures. Design them for a night city: wet asphalt reflections, headlight rim-light, canal mist. La Llorona and Charro Negro should read as silhouettes first; Axolume is the bright counterweight (canal-pink, translucent gills, festival glow). Coatimano and Chupacabra are the street-level animals with market-scrap accessories.

## Landmarks to draw from
Great Plaza · Canal Gardens (trajineras) · Museum Mile · Old Stones Quarter · Reforma Walk · Mercado Mayor · Arena Lucha · Skyline Lookout · Xochival Flower Market

## Guardrails
- Flowers are structural, not decorative — an arch, a boat, an altar should be *built* out of them.
- Keep the ghost stories affectionate and folkloric rather than gory.
- Do not merge with Eagle Serpent Dominion: Xochimex has metro trains, murals, and neon; ESD has banners and canoes.
