## Design thesis
**Law wears a tabard and carries a ledger.** Rheingard is a city that measures things — tolls, hours, flood levels, alms — and then decorates the measuring instruments. Half stone and bell, half oak and guildmark. Angels and account books share a shelf.

## Motifs and symbols
- **The guild sign** — a hanging iron bracket with a craft tool silhouette (the single most reusable asset here)
- Ledger and quill crossed like a coat of arms; the Ledger Knights' short sword beside a long ink list
- Bell iconography with a ring-count code: triple ring = fire, steady roll = flood
- Brass-bound *Gospel of Law* on a lectern
- Bridge-chapel and mill-wheel under an arch
- Chalked pride-marks on apprentice doorsteps

## Pattern and ornament
- Half-timber framing as pattern: straight verticals, cross-braces, and the Andreas cross panel
- Heraldic quartering and diaper (small repeating lozenge fill) on banners and cloth
- Blackletter lettering on notices; carved capitals on stone
- Rose-window tracery and pointed arcading for anything sacred
- Roof slate laid in scallop or fish-scale courses

## Materials and finish
Cut stone, oak framing, lime plaster, slate, hammered iron grilles, brass, pewter tankards, wool, stained glass, candle wax. Finish is **maintained and squared** — chamfered edges, joins that fit, and a faint smell of ink and malt.

## Architecture and silhouette
Steep pointed roofs, bridge towers with iron grilles, a bastioned gate with a toll court, houses built like laced boots along the great bridge, a cathedral close. The skyline is **spiky and vertical**; the market square is a wide flat counterweight.

## Dress and accessories
Layered doublets and gowns, guild colours at the sleeve, a tabard for anyone official. Belt pouches, ink horns, wax seals on cords, key rings, and hats you must remove at the hour bell. Rank is legible: Master, Brother/Sister, Captain, Canon — put the sign of it on the clothing.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Tollbooks posted at noon, floodbooks read aloud, guild plaques, pretzels the size of shields, dark copper-brown beer, monk cheese with caraway, scratch tickets and sweets at *Karl's grosser Kiosk*. Signage is **blackletter on a painted board with a wrought-iron bracket**.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 3`): **Angel, Bullywug, Clockhare, Coin Magpie, Demon, Elf, Iron Kobold, Mailtoad, Mooskuh, Papillon, Reliquary Dove, Stagel, Succubus**.

The widest tonal range of any region: cathedral celestials, guild-workshop constructs, and river vermin. Unify them with **civic hardware** — a wax seal, a tally tag, a small brass bell, a stamped guild mark somewhere on the design. Clockhare, Iron Kobold, and Mailtoad are the workshop line (gears, rivets, satchels); Angel, Reliquary Dove, and Demon are the cathedral line (gilt, glass, censer chains).

## Guardrails
- Rheingard is **ruled and painted**, not crooked and mossy — that is what separates it from Bretonreach.
- Keep the ledger joke visible: even the criminal Tollring gets audited. Paperwork is a design element.
- Downriver talk of Frankenondermeer should always be a slight lowering of the voice, visually a fog bank at the edge of frame.
