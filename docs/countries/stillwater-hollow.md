> **Content note:** this is the setting's deliberate horror locale. Its themes (inbreeding, abuse, contamination, things born wrong) are handled in-lore by implication and silence, never by gore. Keep art on the *implication* side.

## Design thesis
**A wound pretending to be a home.** Nothing here is spectacular; everything is slightly wrong at second glance. The design rule is **subtraction**: a clean patch on a dusty shelf, a scraped-away face in a photograph, a door with scratches only near the floor. The horror is what has been removed or refuses to move.

## Motifs and symbols
- **The still field** — grass that does not move even when wind does
- Family portrait with faces scraped away, hands still on shoulders
- Locked nursery door with scratches low on the paint
- Hoofprints outside a bedroom window, always facing the house
- A well bucket that comes up cold and tastes flat
- A porch light that turns on behind you
- Cracked-voice church bell; a road with no sign

## Pattern and ornament
- Faded floral wallpaper and oilcloth prints, repeated past the point of comfort
- Quilt blocks with one square obviously replaced
- Hand-lettered paper warnings taped in shop windows
- Peeling paint layers exposing older colours underneath
- Sun-bleached signage with letters missing

## Materials and finish
Weathered clapboard, rusted tin roofing, galvanised buckets, oilcloth, cracked linoleum, cheap pine, chicken wire, sealed tins, duct tape. Finish: **dry, dusty, and stopped** — nothing is wet, nothing is growing, nothing has been repaired recently except one thing, badly.

## Architecture and silhouette
Leaning barns, shuttered porches, a church with a cracked bell, a milking shed, a town well with a descent beneath it, and one shop with its light still on. Silhouette: **low, wide, and unlit**, with a single lit window.

## Dress and accessories
Plain, homemade, and out of date by fifty years: stiff Sunday clothes, aprons, work boots, hats indoors. Nothing new, nothing branded, everything mended. Accessories: a bucket, a lantern, a rifle leaning by a door, a filter for the well.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Well filters, barn hardware, sealed tins, paper warnings (`?pg=stcr-shop`), milking pails (`?pg=harmontide-milking-minigame`), lullaby music boxes. Signage is **hand-lettered and apologetic**, or missing entirely.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 29`): **Bloodhound, Hand, Mary, Spewslug, Zombie**.

Deliberately un-fantastic names for deliberately wrong things. Keep silhouettes **almost normal**: Bloodhound is a dog that is a little too calm; Hand is exactly what it says and should never be shown whole; Mary is the thing at the bottom of the well and should stay mostly unseen. Contamination is the visual through-line — pale growths, wrong proportions, too many joints — implied by shadow and framing.

## Landmarks to draw from
Town Well Descent (`?pg=stcr-adventure`) · Stillwater Crossroads Supply · Stillwater Creek Milking Station

## Guardrails
- **Implication over gore.** The lore never shows the thing; neither should the art.
- No jump-scare visual grammar. The dread is stillness and daylight.
- Keep the one wrong-bright colour rare — it is the tell, so it must not become the theme.
