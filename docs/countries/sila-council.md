> **Respect note:** modelled on living Inuit and circumpolar communities. The lore already gets the manners right — Elders first, ask before photos, never photograph a hunt without permission. In art: **invent the motifs**, do not reproduce specific real clothing designs or sacred objects, and never render the community as historical or vanishing. This is a modern town.

## Design thesis
**Weather is teacher and law.** Qilaktuk listens hard, plans long, and keeps the lamps filled. Everything is designed against cold and for sharing: colour-bright houses so you can find them in whiteout, boardwalks over permafrost, sleds parked nose-out, and extra mitts in the warden's pocket.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Qulliq** — the memorial oil lamp, lit for first ice, first thaw, and remembrance. The country's heart-icon.
- **Inuksuk** — stone markers standing like careful people on the headland
- Qamutik sled parked nose-out; snowmachine tracks in fresh snow
- Flagged ice road (do not move a flag)
- VHF radio and the community notice board
- The weekly sealift crate — fuel, flour, guitar strings, hockey tape
- Aurora camera at the heated shelter on the ridge

## Pattern and ornament
- Bright flat colour blocking on buildings, no ornament needed
- Parka trim banding: contrast strips at hem, cuff, and hood (invent the motif set)
- Reflective stripe geometry on gear and outerwear
- Tape-patched wall maps — repair as decoration
- Ice texture: pressure-ridge lines, wind-scour ripples, snow drift curves

## Materials and finish
Painted plywood and metal siding, boardwalk timber, hide and fur trim, modern synthetics, rope, fuel drums, cast iron, stone. Finish: **weather-beaten but well-kept** — paint chalky, edges rimed, everything either tied down or brought inside.

## Architecture and silhouette
Colour-bright houses on a low ridge around a sheltered cove, boardwalks stitching neighbourhoods over permafrost, a short pier, a community hall with a radio room, a heated shelter on the ridge. Silhouette: **small bright boxes on stilts under an enormous sky**, with sleds and drums in every yard.

## Dress and accessories
Serious cold-weather layering: hooded parkas with fur ruffs, wind pants, heavy mitts, goggles, boots. The Ice Ward wears reflective stripes and carries spare mitts for kids. Accessories: radio, thermos, headlamp, spare batteries (batteries lie in the cold).

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Arctic char drying, bannock in cast iron, muskox stew, cloudberries by the mug, tea with condensed milk, fuel cans, sled runners and rope from Outfitter Row, the cold-store goods at Qilaktuk Ice Cache. Signage is **plain painted board plus a taped notice**, plus the all-important conditions board at the ice road gate.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 16`): **Amarok, Aurorantler, Halberelk, Keelut, Moonseal, Penguin, Polar Bear, Terniq**.

Ice fauna plus two northern spirits. Amarok and Keelut are the dangerous ones — keep them **mostly weather**: shape suggested by drifting snow, eyes last. Aurorantler carries the identity colour as light rather than pigment (antlers as aurora curtains). Moonseal and Polar Bear should be accurate and heavy; give the domesticated ones harness, reflective trim, and mitten-coloured gear.

> Note: `Penguin` sits in an Arctic roster, which is a real-world impossibility. That is either a deliberate joke or a data slip — worth a decision either way.

## Guardrails
- **Contemporary, not historical.** Radios, snowmachines, hockey tape, a clinic with a sat link.
- Warmth is a design subject: steam, lamp glow, condensation on a window, a door closed quickly.
- Elders lead. If a scene has a decision in it, an Elder is in the frame.
