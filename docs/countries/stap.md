> **Respect note:** this country is modelled on living Indigenous nations. The lore already sets the rules — consensus by circle, ask before photographing ceremony, restricted knowledge stays restricted. Art direction should follow the same rules: **invent in-world designs, never reproduce real clan designs, sacred regalia, or ceremonial items.** Do not use a war bonnet as a costume piece.

## Design thesis
**Roads follow stars before they follow stakes.** Turtlestar is laid out in rings rather than blocks, and every design decision should express that: circles, shared centres, seating that faces inward, and a horizon that is always visible. Old and new sit together without irony — beadwork beside solar panels, horse lines beside bike racks.

## Motifs and symbols
- **The circle** — council rings, fire at the centre, camp layout, UI framing
- Star Walk: a path laid out to mirror a northern constellation, marked by story poles
- **Winter count** — a painted hide or canvas recording each year with one image (a superb quest/journal UI device)
- Central fire tended by rota; banners planted in the ground, never hung on walls
- Trading blanket laid out with goods on it
- Posted routes and donation lists on a community board
- Managed herd behind a timber gate; controlled burns as seasonal care

## Pattern and ornament
- Geometric beadwork and quillwork bands — invent motif vocabularies rather than copying real ones
- Blanket-stripe banding (broad bands, hard edges)
- Painted-hide narrative art: small figures in rows across a light ground
- Parfleche-style painted rawhide geometry on containers
- Star and constellation layouts used as ground plans and floor inlays

## Materials and finish
Tanned hide, canvas, timber, glass beads, porcupine quill, clay, wood, worked metal, and modern materials sitting alongside — radios, solar panels, aluminium poles. Finish: **honest and functional**, wind-worn, mended rather than replaced.

## Architecture and silhouette
Timber council houses and canvas lodges sharing a wind, an open-roof observatory with windbreak walls, a timber overlook gate, craft rings, a stone-ribbed river ford. Silhouette: **low cones and long horizontals under an enormous sky** — the sky should occupy most of the frame.

## Dress and accessories
Contemporary clothing with regalia kept for ceremony (and drawn only with care). Everyday: work jackets, ribbon shirts and skirts, beaded accessories, wide-brim hats, boots. Trail Wardens wear red sashes and carry a whistle, a water jug, and a radio.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Wild rice, corn, beans, squash, bison jerky, chokecherry pudding, frybread, drums and flutes, clapstick-and-bead craft, coffee shared from a big pot, the Turtlestar Trading Blanket. Signage is **plain posted lettering plus painted symbol boards**, prices posted openly.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 25`): **Alisnapor, Dustbison, Famine, Horned Serpent Uktena, Kachina, Peacepipe Bison, Prairhorn, Thunderbird**.

Plains fauna and sky powers. Thunderbird and Horned Serpent are the elemental pair — storm above, water below — and should be drawn as weather rather than as animals. Dustbison and Prairhorn are the herd line: use blanket-stripe and painted-hide markings. **Kachina and Peacepipe Bison are the sensitive ones**: keep their designs clearly invented and avoid mimicking real ceremonial objects or regalia; a pipe should not be a prop.

## Guardrails
- **Circles, not grids.** If a layout has right-angle blocks, it is wrong for Turtlestar.
- Contemporary and sovereign — never a "vanished people" framing.
- Sacred content stays off-screen. If it would need permission in the lore, it needs to stay out of the art.
