## Design thesis
**Law argued with learning; kindness counted as much as coin.** Shalemdor is pale stone and shade lines — a city literally built around where the sun will be. Its two signature objects are the *page* and the *lamp*: study and light, repeated everywhere from doorframes to festivals.

## Motifs and symbols
- **Clay oil lamp** — the country's core icon; a whole street lights at once at dusk
- Small prayer-scroll case at a doorway lintel, touched lightly in passing
- Braided loaf for the rest day
- Ram horn sounded from a gate tower
- Public scales at the Hall of Scales; weekly charity records posted on a wall
- Olive tree leaning over a cistern; the tiered spring with stone basins
- Wide margins on a page — disputes are written into them, not shouted

## Pattern and ornament
- Ashlar coursing: neat rectangular stone blocks are the base pattern of everything
- Woven canopy stripes over the market lanes
- Seven-branch and lamp-row arrangements as compositional rhythm
- Mosaic floor bands with vine, pomegranate, and geometric guilloche
- Hand-lettered scribal columns with decorated initial words

## Materials and finish
Pale limestone, lime plaster, clay lamps, olive wood, copper, wool, glass beads, parchment, woven canopy cloth. Finish is **sun-warmed and softened by handling** — corners rounded by generations, stone polished where hands rest.

## Architecture and silhouette
Lanes curling between courtyards, vaulted gates, market arcades, joined rooftops you can walk across, domes and chimneys and fig trees in the skyline. Silhouette: **stacked cubes and low domes climbing a ridge**.

## Dress and accessories
Layered linen and wool, covered shoulders in study quarters, head coverings common for all. Accessories: a satchel of pages, an ink pot, a lamp with a carrying loop, prayer fringes, a copper water cup. Olive Ward wardens carry lamp-count ledgers.

## Objects, signage, item flavour
Braided and flat breads, olive oil jars, chickpea and sesame dishes, date cakes, glass beads, copper trays, parchment, charity boxes at every counter, the goods at Olive Lamp Goods. Signage is **hand-lettered on stone plaques or cloth**, often with a proverb attached.

## Creature design cues
Native species (`region_id = 14`): **Ark Seraph, Datebat, Dolphin, Golem, Lilith, Rockhyrax, Ziz**.

Scripture-scale beings alongside small desert-hill animals. Golem is the anchor: clay body with an inscribed word plate, seams still showing the maker's hands. Ark Seraph and Ziz should be architectural-scale and mostly wing; Lilith is the night-side figure — keep her regal rather than monstrous. Rockhyrax and Datebat are the everyday creatures with lamp-oil and date-market accessories.

## Guardrails
- The lore describes a **study-and-hospitality** city; the continent card's "wrapped in controversy" line is a card joke, not the art direction. Do not build real-world political imagery into it.
- Use invented lettering. Never render real liturgical text as decoration or on items.
- Shade is the design tool: every exterior scene should have a hard shade line laid by the architecture.
